The right focus session length depends on the friction of starting your task, the depth of attention it requires, and your current energy. As a general guideline, pick 25 minutes for resisted tasks or low energy, 50 minutes for standard professional work, and 75 minutes for immersive projects like coding or drafting.

**TL;DR**
- 25 minutes (21 min quiet focus): Best for overcoming procrastination, quick admin, or working when energy is low.
- 50 minutes (44 min quiet focus): The balanced default for reports, studying, and routine desk work.
- 75 minutes (69 min quiet focus): Reserved for deep immersion without interruptions, such as software development, heavy research, or long-form writing.

## How session phases work on {app}

Every focus session on {app} follows a consistent three-part structure. You begin with a brief check-in to state your goal, work in silence with microphones muted, and finish with a short wrap-up. The exact division of minutes shifts slightly based on your chosen length.

In a 25-minute session, you spend 2 minutes introducing yourself and stating your task. The quiet focus phase lasts 21 minutes, followed by a 2-minute wrap-up where you share your outcome. This brisk format helps you begin before hesitation takes hold.

In 50-minute and 75-minute sessions, the structure expands to a 3-minute introduction and a 3-minute wrap-up. That leaves 44 minutes of quiet focus in a 50-minute booking, and 69 minutes in a 75-minute booking. You can review the exact phase breakdown on our [how it works](/how-it-works) page.

Sessions start on the hour or half hour, and you are matched with a partner five minutes before the scheduled start. Microphones mute automatically during quiet focus to keep background noise low, though you can unmute whenever necessary.

## The 25-minute session: lowering friction and getting started

The 25-minute session is the most approachable option on the schedule. It mirrors the classic interval popularized by Francesco Cirillo in the Pomodoro Technique. Twenty-five minutes feels brief enough that your mind rarely puts up heavy resistance before you begin.

This duration is ideal for tasks you have been actively avoiding. When an assignment feels tedious or overwhelming, agreeing to work on it for 21 quiet minutes lowers the emotional hurdle. You do not need to finish the entire project; you only need to show up for one short interval.

Short sessions also suit discrete administrative chores like clearing an inbox, sorting receipts, or scheduling appointments. Because the quiet phase is compact, you stay on track without drifting toward unrelated tabs. A 25-minute block is also the safest choice when fatigue or brain fog makes longer commitments feel daunting.

## The 50-minute session: the balanced everyday default

Fifty minutes is the standard session length for good reason. It provides a generous 44-minute focus window, giving you enough time to settle into an idea without feeling rushed. Most [remote workers](/body-doubling-for/remote-workers) find this duration fits naturally into a typical workday calendar.

This length aligns with implementation intentions research by psychologist Peter Gollwitzer. When you declare a concrete plan—such as stating that you will draft a specific memo—44 minutes provides the room needed to turn that intention into reality. The goal feels substantial, and the session boundary keeps you moving forward.

A 50-minute block works well for drafting documents, reviewing spreadsheets, or preparing presentations. Because sessions start on the hour or half hour, a 50-minute session leaves a built-in 10-minute cushion before the next hour begins. That window lets you stretch, grab fresh water, and rest your eyes before your next task.

## The 75-minute session: sustained depth for immersion

The 75-minute session is designed for demanding cognitive work that requires prolonged immersion. Getting into a deep train of thought often takes ten to fifteen minutes of mental ramp-up. A longer block gives you 69 minutes of quiet focus, ensuring you do not get interrupted right when your thinking becomes fluid.

Software developers, researchers, and [writers](/body-doubling-for/writers) benefit heavily from this extended duration. If you are architecting a database schema, debugging code, or drafting a complex argument, frequent stops break your concentration. The 75-minute session protects your focus and allows you to resolve difficult problems in one sitting.

This format draws on social facilitation research dating back to Norman Triplett in 1898 showing that people often perform tasks differently in the presence of others. Working quietly alongside a video partner for over an hour creates a steady anchor that discourages casual distractions. Because it requires significant stamina, reserve 75-minute bookings for high-energy mornings or dedicated project days.

## Matching session length to your energy and the day

Your energy is rarely constant throughout the week. A session length that felt effortless on Tuesday morning might feel impossible by Thursday afternoon. Choosing the right length requires listening to your body and matching the time commitment to your real capacity.

During peak alertness hours, longer sessions of 50 or 75 minutes make the best use of your attention. When the afternoon lull arrives, switch your strategy toward shorter sessions. Completing two distinct 25-minute blocks with a real pause between them is far more productive than staring blankly at a screen during a longer session.

For neurodivergent professionals, especially those with ADHD, attention can vary widely from hour to hour. On days when task initiation feels paralyzed, booking a 25-minute block lowers the barrier to entry and helps establish initial traction. Many members in our [ADHD body doubling](/body-doubling-for/adhd) community use 25-minute sessions as a gentle ramp into the workday.

Keep in mind that body doubling can help some people organize their attention and follow through on intentions. However, it is not a medical treatment or a substitute for professional care when health is involved. Use it as an environmental support alongside whatever other habits and treatments serve you best.

## How breaks fit between your focus blocks

Focusing with a partner is effective, but your brain requires rest to consolidate information and replenish attention. Stacking sessions back-to-back without leaving your desk can cause fatigue to accumulate rapidly. Incorporating deliberate breaks between bookings keeps your concentration steady throughout the day.

Because {app} sessions start on the hour and half hour, natural rest periods are built into the schedule. A 25-minute session leaves a 5-minute break before the next half-hour mark. A 50-minute session leaves a 10-minute break before the next full hour starts.

Use these intervals away from screens to stand up, walk around, or look out a window. Stepping away physically signals to your nervous system that the work sprint has concluded, making it easier to re-engage when the next session starts. Tracking your weekly streaks and total focus hours on your profile can help you observe which pacing works best for your schedule.

## A step-by-step guide to choosing your next session

Before booking your next slot, run through this four-step check to decide which duration fits your current situation. Taking thirty seconds to assess your task prevents you from overcommitting or cutting your work short.

Step 1: Gauge your emotional resistance to the task. If you feel strong hesitation or anxiety, pick a 25-minute session immediately. Lowering the threshold to act is far more important than attempting a long stretch of work.

Step 2: Examine the complexity of the assignment. If the task requires deep context, multiple references, and uninterrupted logic, choose a 75-minute session. If the work is standard and well-defined, choose 50 minutes.

Step 3: Check your remaining physical energy. If you slept poorly or have been in meetings all day, downgrade your planned session to 25 or 50 minutes. Giving yourself a gentler container prevents frustration and helps you finish the day on a positive note.

Step 4: Book your slot and define your opening statement. Choose a booking that aligns with your calendar, and write down exactly what you will say to your partner during the introduction. ## FAQ

### Which session length is best for a first-time user?

A 25-minute session is usually the best starting point for your first video coworking experience. It gives you a quick introduction to the format, lets you test your setup, and allows you to experience the intro and wrap-up without a heavy time commitment. Once you feel comfortable with the rhythm, you can easily graduate to 50-minute blocks.

### What should I do if I finish my task before the session ends?

If you finish your stated goal early, you can spend the remaining quiet time reviewing your work or transitioning to a small backup task. When you state your goal during the intro, it often helps to mention a secondary chore just in case you work faster than expected. During the wrap-up, simply mark your task as done and let your partner know that you completed both items.

### Can I talk to my partner during the quiet focus period?

Microphones mute automatically once the focus period begins so that both people can concentrate in silence. If an urgent question or technical glitch arises, you can unmute briefly or send a quick note in the chat box. Otherwise, you should preserve the quiet environment until the timer signals the wrap-up check-in.

This flexibility allows you to balance spontaneous partner matching with familiar accountability.
