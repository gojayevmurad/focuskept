Virtual coworking provides structured accountability from home over video, while a physical coworking space offers a dedicated desk and an out-of-the-house routine. The right choice depends on whether your main challenge is staying on task at your computer or escaping the isolation of working from home.

TL;DR:
- Virtual coworking pairs you with an accountability partner over video to complete focused work sessions from anywhere.
- Physical coworking spaces offer a shared office, in-person social contact, and a clear physical separation between work and home.
- Many remote workers combine both by using an office for variety and virtual sessions for disciplined execution.

Working remotely offers flexibility over your schedule, but it also removes the physical boundaries of an office. Without coworkers nearby, minor interruptions at home easily turn into hours of procrastination. Rebuilding that structure usually comes down to choosing between a digital accountability tool and a local shared workspace.

## What is virtual coworking?

Virtual coworking brings the experience of working alongside a peer into a structured video call. Instead of commuting, you join a video session from your desk to share a dedicated block of time with a partner. Both participants work independently on their own separate projects rather than collaborating.

On {app}, sessions last for 25, 50, or 75 minutes and start on the hour or half hour. You reserve a time slot, and the system matches you with a partner five minutes before the start. You can also join hosted group sessions or participate in communities designed for study groups and teams.

Every 1:1 session follows a clear three-part rhythm. During the first two to three minutes, you keep your camera on and state your goal to your partner. Afterward, microphones mute automatically for quiet work until a short wrap-up check-in where you mark your task as done, partly done, or not done.

This format draws on the concept of body doubling, where the quiet presence of another person makes starting and sustaining difficult tasks easier. While body doubling helps many people build momentum, it is not a medical treatment or a substitute for professional care when health is involved. It functions as a practical framework for self-regulation and focus.

## The psychology behind video accountability

Virtual coworking relies on two well-established psychological concepts to support personal discipline. The first is social facilitation, tracing back to research by Norman Triplett in 1898 showing that people often apply more sustained effort in the presence of others. Seeing another person quietly working on screen provides a subtle cue to stay on task.

The second concept involves implementation intentions, studied by psychologist Peter Gollwitzer. Stating an explicit plan before starting—such as declaring that you will draft one report section—makes you far more likely to follow through. The wrap-up check-in reinforces that commitment with an immediate moment of closure.

The set time limits also draw on the Pomodoro Technique, developed by Francesco Cirillo. Working in 25-minute intervals, or extending to 50 or 75 minutes, gives your mind a clear finish line.

## What is a physical coworking space?

A physical coworking space is a commercial office where freelancers, remote employees, and entrepreneurs rent workspace alongside one another. Rather than leasing a private building, members share common infrastructure like internet, phone booths, meeting rooms, and kitchens. Memberships generally offer hot desks, dedicated desks, or small offices.

The main advantage of a physical space is the geographic separation between home life and professional life. Leaving your house creates a distinct mental transition into work mode. For anyone struggling to switch off at the end of the day, that commute establishes a helpful boundary.

## Comparing virtual and physical coworking

Deciding between these two options requires looking closely at how each one affects your budget, your attention span, your relationships, and your calendar. Each model addresses different vulnerabilities of independent work.

### 1. Cost and overhead

Physical coworking spaces require a regular financial commitment. Shared desk memberships typically cost several hundred dollars each month, in addition to commuting expenses and purchased meals.

Virtual coworking removes almost all financial overhead by using your existing home setup.

On {app}, you can begin on a Free plan offering three focus sessions per week. An optional Plus plan is available for unlimited bookings, while the codebase remains open source under the AGPL-3.0 license.

### 2. Focus and deep work

Physical offices provide an energetic atmosphere, but they also bring noise. Background chatter, phone calls, and movement across the room create frequent distractions.

Virtual coworking is built around silence. Microphones mute automatically during the working block, keeping visual company without background noise.

Knowing that you will review your progress with your partner in 50 minutes also prevents task switching. The defined duration gives you a clear horizon to sustain concentration.

### 3. Social contact and community

A physical space offers spontaneous in-person interaction throughout the workday. Having a quick chat by the kettle or sharing lunch helps reduce the isolation of working alone.

Virtual coworking provides structured social presence without conversational drift. Your interactions are polite and confined to the check-in windows.

For broader community, {app} provides monthly challenges, weekly streaks, team communities, and the option to favourite partners to book through their personal `/u/<username>` link.

### 4. Flexibility and routine

Visiting an office requires packing gear, commuting through traffic, and adhering to transit schedules. Those travel demands can consume hours when juggling family obligations or busy days.

Virtual coworking fits directly into your daily flow. You can join a session from any desk, at home or while traveling.

Sessions start on the hour and half hour throughout the day. If a gap appears in your afternoon, you can book a partner five minutes beforehand and start working immediately.

## When virtual coworking wins

Virtual coworking is best when your main struggle is task initiation. It creates immediate external urgency for tasks that you might otherwise delay, like clearing an inbox, editing a manuscript, or writing code.

It is especially valuable for [remote workers](/body-doubling-for/remote-workers) who have a comfortable home setup but lose momentum in the middle of the day. A scheduled session at 2:00 PM stops an afternoon slump from stretching into wasted hours.

It is also the practical choice for workers on a tight budget or those balancing work with family routines. You gain the accountability of an office without commuting time or monthly membership fees.

## When a physical coworking space wins

A physical coworking space is the better option when your living situation disrupts your concentration. If your home is cramped, loud, or lacks reliable internet, renting a desk outside is worth the investment.

It also helps when staying indoors for days harms your motivation. The sensory routine of leaving home, walking outside, and seeing other people in person can renew your energy.

Finally, physical spaces are necessary if your work involves hosting clients in person, leading workshops, or collaborating directly with local colleagues. Formal meeting rooms provide professional credibility that a home office cannot replicate.

## How to combine both methods for maximum output

You do not have to view virtual coworking and physical spaces as competing alternatives. Many remote professionals combine both models into a weekly routine that balances deep focus with social variety.

### Step 1: Dedicate office days to collaborative or exploratory work

Reserve one or two days each week for a physical coworking space or quiet library. Use that time for reading, outlining, attending calls, and connecting with peers over coffee. Leaving deep analytical tasks for home keeps open-office noise from interrupting your hardest work.

### Step 2: Anchor your desk time with virtual sessions

If you find yourself distracted while sitting at a hot desk, combine both approaches. Put on headphones, open {app}, and book a 50-minute virtual session. The combination of an office setting and a video accountability partner creates an effective defense against procrastination.

### Step 3: Track your weekly focus hours

Review your output to see where you actually get work done. On {app}, you can track weekly streaks and accumulated focus hours to evaluate your consistency. Comparing productive hours across locations helps you decide whether a paid desk membership is truly worth the cost.

## FAQ

### Can I use virtual coworking while working from a physical coworking space?

Yes, you can join a virtual session from any workspace with a stable internet connection. Wear headphones so you can complete the intro and wrap-up quietly without bothering people around you. Keep your microphone muted during the focus period so background office noise never carries through.

### Is virtual coworking the same as body doubling?

Virtual coworking is an online form of body doubling. Working alongside an accountability partner provides the quiet company needed to stay on task. It is a practical routine, though it is not a medical treatment or a substitute for professional care.

### How do I choose between a 25, 50, or 75-minute session on {app}?

A 25-minute session works best for quick administrative chores or breaking initial resistance on a dreaded task. A 50-minute session is the standard choice for writing, coding, or sustained research. A 75-minute session suits deep work blocks when you already have momentum.

### What should I do if I finish my task before the session ends?

If you finish early, remain in the session until the scheduled check-in. Use the remaining minutes for a secondary task like organizing notes or planning your next step. When the session ends, mark your task as done and share your progress with your partner.
